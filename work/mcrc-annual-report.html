Turning a year of research into a story worth reading

The brief

The Murugappa Chettiar Research Centre (MCRC) needed its 2023–24 Annual Report to cover a full year of work, from lab research to programs in villages, for readers ranging from funders to the communities themselves. That came with four challenges.

  • A lot of ground to cover. 15 research programs and 13 community outreach programs.
  • Technical material. Scientific research that had to make sense to non-specialists.
  • Many authors. Drafts from different scholars, each with their own style.
  • Mixed audiences. Funders, government bodies, researchers, and the public, all in one document.

What I did

  1. Shaped the structure. Proposed an arc that moves from research to community impact to the beneficiaries' own voices, across eight sections.
  2. Framed it around the FAO's Four Betters. Opened the report with Better Production, Nutrition, Environment, and Life, giving 28 programs a shared lens.
  3. Interviewed the scholars. Spoke with researchers to understand their work before writing about it.
  4. Edited the research for clarity. Turned technical drafts into plain language, with several review rounds to keep every detail accurate.
  5. Wrote the connecting narrative. About Us, Key Highlights, section introductions, Voice of Beneficiaries, Moments and Milestones, the acknowledgment, and the reflective lines at the foot of each page.
  6. Unified the voice. Brought many authors into one consistent tone from cover to close.

The result

  • 15research programs made readable for a general audience.
  • 13community outreach programs documented.
  • 64pages, published as MCRC's 2023–24 Annual Report.

What I learned: Making research readable isn't about leaving things out. It's about finding the plainest true sentence, then checking it with the person who did the work until it's both accurate and easy to follow. Working this way with MCRC's scholars taught me that clarity and credibility come from the same place: understanding the work well enough to explain it simply.
